A by AMC · Audiotherm development concept

The same thinking.
In the kitchen.

In my work on the AMC cooking concept, I’m asking the same question I ask in fitness: how can a physical product help someone understand what is happening and know what to do next? Here, that thinking becomes a sensor in the lid and a clear cue for the cook.

Understanding at the product.

The lid works out the cooking state. The ring communicates it through a simple visual cue. “Now” means a cooking window; the cook still checks the food and adjusts the heat.

One signal. Optional connections.

The same state and method could reach a countertop controller, an app, or compatible partner devices. The standalone ring remains the basic experience.

Development concept. Optional connections require compatibility and safety testing; products and partner integrations are not confirmed.

Where I see the market heading

The interface can change. The value comes from the devices, data, and understanding underneath it.

My view: software becomes the utility.

I believe building software will keep getting easier. More value will sit in reliable device connections, useful data, and proprietary intelligence. Most people will want an experience that already works, rather than building the system themselves.

Knowing what to connect, what it means, and how it fits into someone’s life—that’s what I bring.
