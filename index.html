01 / A life in motion

The body has always
been the starting point.

Movement is something I study, something I build around, and something I practice. Exercise physiology gave me a way to understand how the body responds and adapts. A lifetime of training keeps that understanding personal.

Standing beside my son at HYROX. Traveling with my wife. Staying capable of saying yes to what comes next. Those are the reasons strength, health, and longevity matter to me.

That same curiosity has shaped my career: how can we help people move, feel, and live better—and make the experience something they want to return to?

02 / What the years have taught me

A wider view.
Built through experience.

My work has taken me from understanding human movement to understanding the products, people, and businesses that surround it.

Working in fitness equipment and product development, including at Technogym, meant thinking about the point where a person meets a machine. Building Functional Solutions brought connected fitness and digital coaching into that picture. Each experience added another way to see the same challenge.

Through Hannum Strategy and Fire and Ice Wellness, those questions remain close to everyday life. A product has to make sense to the person using it. A service has to earn someone’s trust. A business has to deliver on what it promises.

That combination shapes my judgment. Physiology helps me understand the body. Product experience helps me understand the interaction. Building and operating businesses keeps me grounded in what people will actually use.

03 / What brings it all together

This is where it
comes together for me.

I’ve spent my life working with the body, the equipment we use, and the businesses built around helping people. Today, I see a chance to bring those parts of my world closer together.

Devices give us a connection to what is happening in the physical world. AI opens new ways to work with that information. My experience in physiology and product development helps me ask what it means for the person—and how to turn that understanding into something useful.

That’s what draws me forward: bringing what I’ve learned into experiences that help people understand their bodies and make more informed choices about how they move, train, and recover.

04 / Where the physical and digital meet

Real-time data.
Understanding in the moment.

Sensors, wearables, devices, and machines connect us to what is happening now. The opportunity is to interpret that data as it arrives—bringing physiology, intelligence, and personal context together to help someone decide what to do next.

Capture→Interpret→Respond
01Capture→

What’s happening now.

Sensors & devices

Signals such as heart rate and cadence help describe the body’s response and movement as they happen.

Wearables

Available live measurements connect the person to the moment. Activity and recovery history add context.

Machines & equipment

Treadmills, bikes, rowers, and connected equipment capture the work: speed, distance, cadence, or power.

02Interpret→

What it means.

Bring incoming data together with the person’s history, goals, and how they feel.

PhysiologyIntelligenceContext

The aim is to interpret changes as they happen: how the body is responding, how effort is changing, and what deserves attention.

03Respond↻

A useful action. Now.

Make the information useful while the person or coach can act on it.

  • Understand the effort as it unfolds.
  • Inform a change in pace, intensity, or rest.
  • See how the body responds to that change.
Software makes the connections work.From live signals to interpretation to useful feedback.
